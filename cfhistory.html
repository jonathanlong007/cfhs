Three Villages Merge Into One

Early settlers in the valley included Native Americans drawn by the Tuscarawas River watershed . Following the Battle of Fallen Timbers and Treaty of Fort Industry , the U.S. surveyed the area in 1800 and 1807 . Pioneers favored the affordable, fertile western bank , leading to the founding of the village of Milan in 1814 .

The Muskingum Watershed drainage pathways connected early settlements to broader continental trade networks.
The Miller Fruit Farm sat at the top of the hill along Arcadia Street.

Inspired by the Erie Canal, Ohio enacted internal improvements . The village of Fulton was laid out in 1826 along the eastern bank , welcoming its first canal boat by 1828 . Growth continued with the 1832 founding of West Fulton , culminating in the 1853 merger of all three villages into a single port . As railroads challenged canals , the town transitioned to regional coal mining with over 20 active mines by the 1880s .

The Great Western Warehouse housed pharmacies, markets, and an upper-floor opera house.
