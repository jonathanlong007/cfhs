A Tale of Three Villages

In 1825, the Ohio State Legislature passed an act that enabled the construction of our first interstate transportation system: the canals.10 Anticipating an immense economic surge, local innovators immediately drew up plans for the village of Fulton in 1826.11

Fulton was laid out to run exactly parallel to this major man-made shipping channel. The gamble paid off gracefully. In August 1828, the inaugural canal boat, the State of Ohio, made its triumphant passage through town, introducing a robust era of inland trade and immigration.12

The Great Western Warehouse hosted bustling canal marketplaces, circa 1848.
